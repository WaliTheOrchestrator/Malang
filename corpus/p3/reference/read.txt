The record is the point. Everything else in this system exists to protect one small, growing archive of conversation, written to disk the moment the words are spoken and never quietly rewritten afterward. A mind can be rented from a company that may meter it, suspend it, or retire it without warning, but the transcript belongs to the person who lived it. So the first thing built is not the voice and not the cleverness; it is the humble, stubborn machinery that makes sure nothing said is ever lost. If the power fails mid-sentence, the sentence survives. If the model changes overnight, the memory does not. That is the whole bet: that custody of the record outlasts the rental of the reasoning, and that patience with the boring parts is what earns the right to the interesting ones later.
